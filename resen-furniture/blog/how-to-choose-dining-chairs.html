Buyer's Guide

How to Choose the Right Dining Chair for Your Business

July 13, 2026 8 min read Product Selection
Home News Choosing Dining Chairs

Whether you're outfitting a restaurant chain, stocking a furniture store, or sourcing for a hotel project, choosing the right dining chair is one of the most consequential decisions you'll make. The chair defines the dining experience — it must be comfortable, durable, visually appealing, and within your budget. This guide walks you through the key considerations every B2B buyer should evaluate before placing an order.

1. Material Selection: Fabric vs PU vs Plastic

The material you choose determines the chair's look, feel, durability, and price point. Each option has distinct advantages depending on your target market and application.

Velvet / Fabric Chairs (HC-R Series)

Velvet and fabric dining chairs offer a premium look and soft touch that appeals to European and Middle Eastern markets. They're ideal for upscale restaurants, hotel dining rooms, and high-end retail. The HC-R series from Resen comes in a wide range of colors and can be fully customized to match any interior design scheme.

Fabric chairs typically require an MOQ of 200 units per model, with lead times of 3–4 weeks for standard colors.

PU Leather Chairs (HC-U Series)

PU leather combines the look of genuine leather with a more affordable price point and easier maintenance. It's the best-selling category for most importers because it works across mid-range to premium segments. PU chairs are wipe-clean, making them practical for restaurants and cafes. Our HC-U series covers everything from classic Parsons-style to modern geometric designs.

Plastic Chairs (HC-P / HC-N Series)

Injection-moulded plastic chairs are lightweight, stackable, and highly cost-effective. They dominate the cafe, event, and budget hospitality segments. With MOQs around 500 units, they're ideal for high-volume buyers. Our HC-P series includes popular designs inspired by mid-century modern classics, while the HC-N series offers more contemporary silhouettes.

2. Frame Construction: What's Under the Upholstery

The frame is the foundation of any good chair. At Resen, we use powder-coated steel tubing for the majority of our dining chair frames. This provides superior structural integrity compared to wooden frames and allows for a slimmer profile without sacrificing strength. All metal frames undergo:

  • Tube bending and cutting — precision CNC-controlled for consistency
  • Welding — MIG welding at key stress points
  • Powder coating — electrostatic application followed by oven curing for a scratch-resistant finish

For wooden-leg chairs, we use solid beech or ash sourced from audited suppliers. Each leg is inspected for grain consistency and moisture content before finishing.

4. Regional Market Preferences

Over six years of exporting to 60+ countries, we've observed clear regional patterns in dining chair preferences:

  • Western Europe: Prefers minimalist, Scandinavian-inspired designs. Fabric and muted colours dominate. Quality certifications (CE marking) are non-negotiable.
  • Eastern Europe: Values strong price-to-quality ratio. PU leather chairs are the top seller. Darker colour palettes — black, dark grey, brown.
  • South America: Bold colours and ornate details perform well. Velvet chairs in jewel tones (emerald, sapphire, ruby) are especially popular for hospitality projects.
  • Middle East: Gold accents, tufted upholstery, and high-back designs. Luxury aesthetic with strong demand for customization.

5. What to Look for in a Supplier

Not all manufacturers are equal. When evaluating a furniture supplier, look for these indicators of reliability:

  1. Factory scale: A larger facility (30,000m²+) typically means better production capacity and shorter lead times during peak seasons.
  2. In-house production lines: Metal, upholstery, and injection moulding under one roof means better quality control and faster turnaround.
  3. Response time: A supplier that responds to inquiries within 2 hours demonstrates operational efficiency.
  4. OEM/ODM capability: Can they manufacture to your specifications? Drawing-based customization is the gold standard.
  5. Quality control process: Ask about their QC checkpoints — incoming materials, in-process, and finished product inspection.
