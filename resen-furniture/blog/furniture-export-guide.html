Furniture Export from China: A Complete Guide for Importers

July 13, 2026 10 min read Logistics & Compliance
Home News Export Guide

Importing furniture from China can unlock significant cost advantages — but only if you navigate the process correctly. From understanding Incoterms and certifications to optimizing container loading and managing customs clearance, there's a lot to learn. This guide draws on our six years of experience exporting Resen Furniture to 60+ countries to help you import with confidence.

1. Understanding FOB Pricing and Incoterms

When you receive a quote from a Chinese manufacturer, it's almost always FOB (Free on Board) pricing. This means the price covers the product plus delivery to the port and loading onto the vessel — but does NOT include ocean freight, insurance, or destination charges.

Common Incoterms for Furniture Export

  • FOB (Free on Board): Seller delivers goods to the named port and loads them onto the vessel. Buyer arranges and pays for ocean freight, insurance, and destination handling. This is the most common Incoterm for furniture.
  • CIF (Cost, Insurance, Freight): Seller covers product cost, insurance, and freight to the destination port. Buyer handles customs clearance and inland delivery.
  • EXW (Ex Works): Buyer collects goods from the factory gate and handles everything. Only recommended if you have an established logistics partner in China.
Pro tip: If you're a first-time importer, FOB is the safest bet. You control the freight forwarder and can shop around for the best ocean freight rates.

2. Certifications and Compliance

Depending on your destination market, your furniture may need to meet specific certification requirements. Here are the key ones to know:

CE Marking (European Union)

CE marking is mandatory for furniture sold in the EU. It certifies that the product meets EU safety, health, and environmental requirements. For dining chairs, this typically involves:

  • Structural stability testing (EN 1022, EN 1728)
  • Flammability testing (BS 5852, EN 1021)
  • Chemical compliance (REACH regulation for materials and finishes)

SGS / Intertek Inspection

Many importers request third-party inspection through SGS, Bureau Veritas, or Intertek. These organizations verify product quality, quantity, and packaging before shipment. At Resen, we welcome third-party inspections at any stage of production — it's a standard part of our export workflow.

Country-Specific Standards

  • USA: TB117-2013 flammability (California), CPSIA for children's products
  • UK: UKCA marking (post-Brexit), BS 5852 flammability
  • Australia/New Zealand: AS/NZS 4688 for furniture stability

3. Container Loading and Optimization

Container space is money. How you pack your furniture determines how many units you ship per container — and therefore your per-unit shipping cost.

Container Sizes

Container Type Internal Dims (L×W×H) Approx. Dining Chairs
20ft GP5.9 × 2.35 × 2.39m~400–500 KD chairs
40ft GP12.03 × 2.35 × 2.39m~900–1,100 KD chairs
40ft HQ12.03 × 2.35 × 2.69m~1,100–1,300 KD chairs

KD = Knocked Down (flat-packed). Assembled chairs take 2–3× more space.

Packaging Standards at Resen

Every Resen chair is packed in export-grade cartons with foam padding, corner reinforcement, and moisture barrier. We use five-layer corrugated cartons rated for 5+ stack levels. For high-value orders, we can add individual polybag wrapping and desiccant packs for humidity protection during ocean transit.

4. Payment Terms and Trade Assurance

Standard payment terms for first-time orders are 30% deposit, 70% balance before shipment. After establishing a relationship, many suppliers offer more flexible terms. We recommend:

  • Alibaba Trade Assurance: Protects your deposit and guarantees on-time shipment. Your payment is held in escrow until you confirm receipt.
  • T/T (Telegraphic Transfer): Most common payment method. 30/70 split is standard.
  • L/C (Letter of Credit): Recommended for large orders ($50,000+). Provides bank-backed payment security but involves more paperwork.

5. Customs Clearance and Documentation

Your freight forwarder will typically handle customs clearance, but you'll need to provide the following documents:

  1. Commercial Invoice — itemized product list with unit prices and total value
  2. Packing List — carton dimensions, weights, and quantities
  3. Bill of Lading (B/L) — issued by the shipping line, serves as title to the goods
  4. Certificate of Origin — often required for preferential tariff rates (e.g., Form A for GSP countries)
  5. Fumigation Certificate — required if wooden packaging materials are used (ISPM 15 compliance)

6. Typical Timeline

Here's what a typical order timeline looks like from inquiry to delivery:

  • Day 1–3: Inquiry received, quote provided, sample photos shared
  • Day 4–14: Order confirmed, deposit paid, production begins
  • Day 15–35: Production (3–4 weeks typical for most orders)
  • Day 36–40: QC inspection, packaging, container loading
  • Day 41–70: Ocean freight (20–30 days depending on destination)
  • Day 71–80: Customs clearance and final delivery

Air freight is available for urgent orders but typically costs 5–8× more than ocean freight.
